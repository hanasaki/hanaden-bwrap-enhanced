# Hanaden SDLC Hierarchy — The Spark and the Engine

> Every great project begins with a spark and runs on an engine.
> This document explains how the six layers of the Hanaden SDLC hierarchy
> map to human motivation, and why that mapping produces systems that endure.

---

## The Two Forces

### 🔥 Motivators — The Sparks

A **motivator** is an **external, short-term impulse** that gets you moving.
It is immediate, tangible, and often circumstantial. It answers:
**"What made me start?"**

Motivators are reactive — they respond to a specific situation:

| Domain | Motivator example |
|--------|-------------------|
| Student | Getting an A on a specific test |
| Employee | Avoiding a penalty or lecture from a manager |
| User | A fun, gamified learning app streak |
| Business | A competitor launching a disruptive product |
| Engineering | A production outage that exposed a weakness |

Motivators are **powerful but fragile**. They get you through the door,
but they do not keep you in the room.

### ⚙️ Drivers — The Engine

A **driver** is an **internal, long-term force** that sustains effort when motivation fades.
It is identity-level, persistent, and self-reinforcing. It answers:
**"Why do I keep going when it gets hard?"**

Drivers are proactive — they persist even without external triggers:

| Domain | Driver example |
|--------|---------------|
| Student | An insatiable curiosity about how the world works |
| Professional | A long-term vision of becoming a medical doctor |
| Builder | A personal standard of never leaving a problem unsolved |
| Business | E-commerce facilities lose $2B annually in worker transit fatigue |
| Engineering | Legacy Wi-Fi drops below −85 dBm in 80% of warehouse aisles |

Drivers are **durable but heavy**. They keep you moving for years,
but they need occasional sparks to stay energised.

---

## How They Work Together

Motivators and drivers are not opposites. They are not substitutes.
They are partners in a self-reinforcing cycle:

```mermaid
flowchart TD
    M["🔥 MOTIVATOR<br/><i>The Spark</i><br/>Gives the initial impulse to start"]:::spark

    R["🔄 REPETITION<br/><i>The Practice</i><br/>You repeat the action over and over"]:::rep

    D["⚙️ DRIVER<br/><i>The Engine</i><br/>The action becomes a habit rooted in identity"]:::engine

    M -->|"ignites"| R
    R -->|"deepens into"| D
    D -->|"recharges from"| M

    classDef engine fill:#2980b9,stroke:#1a5276,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
```

### 1. Motivators Introduce You to Drivers

You rarely start a hard journey with a deep internal driver.
You start with a simple motivator.

> **Example:** You go to the gym because you want to look good for a summer
> vacation *(motivator)*. While working out, you discover you love the feeling
> of strength. Over time, that external goal transforms into an internal need
> for physical resilience *(driver)*.

### 2. Drivers Sustain You When Motivators Die

Motivators are unreliable. They disappear when you are tired, sick, or stressed.
When the motivator fades, the driver takes over.

> **Example:** You start a business because you see a video about making fast money
> *(motivator)*. The business gets hard, and you lose money. If you stay, it is
> because you have a deeper drive to build something of your own and prove your
> capability *(driver)*.

### 3. Motivators Recharge Drivers

Even the most driven people get exhausted. When a long-term driver feels heavy,
a short-term motivator provides a quick burst of energy.

> **Example:** A writer driven by a love for storytelling feels burned out. Winning
> a small local writing contest *(motivator)* gives them the quick burst of joy
> needed to keep writing their big novel.

## Mapping to the SDLC Hierarchy

The spark-and-engine cycle maps directly to the Hanaden document hierarchy.
Each layer in the SDLC has a behavioural role:

### The Behavioural Role of Each Layer

| Layer | Node Type | Behavioural Role | Analogy | Question it answers |
|-------|-----------|------------------|---------|---------------------|
| 0 | CORP-MISSION / CORP-STRAT | **The Identity** | Who you are and what you are aiming for | "Why do we exist?" |
| 1 | B-DRIV / T-DRIV | **The Engine** | The deep, persistent problem that sustains effort | "Why must we change?" |
| 1 | B-MOTI / T-MOTI | **The Spark** | The proposed solution that gets everyone moving | "What will we do about it?" |
| 2 | FEAT | **The Action** | The concrete capability you build | "What does the system DO?" |
| 3 | ARCH | **The Blueprint** | The structural contract — components, data flow, lifecycle | "How do the parts fit together?" |
| 3–4 | SPEC | **The Measurement** | The testable constraint that proves correctness | "How do we prove it works?" |
| — | Primitive | **The Truth** | The terminal value — no further decomposition | "What is the number?" |

1. **The Engine persists:** Even when the team is tired of drones, the $2B transit fatigue

2. **The Spark re-energises:** A successful hot-swap test proves 25.0 kg payload capacity
   *(Spec → Leaf)*. That measurable win *(Motivator)* recharges the team for the next sprint.
3. **The Action compounds:** Each completed Feature makes the next Feature easier to build.
   The system grows organically because the engine and sparks reinforce each other.

## Structural Rules — How the Tree Holds Together

Every relationship in the tree uses a single mechanism: **child→parent pointer**.

```mermaid
flowchart BT
    SP["📐 SPEC<br/>parent → ARCH"]:::spec
    ARCH["🏗️ ARCH<br/>parent → FEAT"]:::arch
    FEAT["🚗 FEAT<br/>parent → MOTI"]:::feat
    MOTI["🔥 MOTI<br/>parent → DRIV"]:::moti
    DRIV["⚙️ DRIV<br/>parent → STRAT"]:::driv
    STRAT["👑 STRAT<br/>(root — no parent)"]:::l0

    SP -->|"parent"| ARCH
    ARCH -->|"parent"| FEAT
    FEAT -->|"parent"| MOTI
    MOTI -->|"parent"| DRIV
    DRIV -->|"parent"| STRAT

    classDef l0   fill:#16a085,stroke:#0e6655,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
    classDef driv fill:#2980b9,stroke:#1a5276,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
    classDef moti fill:#e74c3c,stroke:#c0392b,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
    classDef feat fill:#e67e22,stroke:#ca6f1e,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
    classDef arch fill:#8e44ad,stroke:#6c3483,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
    classDef spec fill:#4a90d9,stroke:#2c5f8a,color:#fff,font-weight:bold
```

| Rule | What it means |
|------|---------------|
| **Single identity** | Every document has one ID: `filename-id` (Hanaden UUIDv7 Extended stem). No `node-id`. |
| **Parent pointer only** | Every non-root node has `parent: <filename-id>`. No `children` lists. |
| **Single identity** | Every document has one ID: `filename-id` (Hanaden UUIDv7 Extended stem). No `node-id`. |
| **Parent pointer only** | Every non-root node has `parent: <filename-id>`. No `children` lists. No `children-order`. No `depends-on`. No `order`. |
| **No sibling pairing** | MOTI is a child of DRIV, not a sibling. The chain is linear: STRAT → DRIV → MOTI → FEAT. |
| **SSoT on the enabler** | When a SPEC unlocks a FEAT, the SPEC declares `unlocks: <FEAT filename-id>`. The FEAT carries nothing. |
| **One pointer format** | `parent`, `unlocks`, `governs`, `supersedes`, `references` — all use `filename-id`. |
| **Directory = parent chain** | Filesystem path mirrors `parent:` chain. `cd ..` walks up one parent pointer. Every directory uses `Slug.class/` naming. |
| **Discover children by directory** | To find a node's children: `ls *.{class}/` in that node's directory. No grep scanning needed. |
| **Every node is a directory** | Even leaf specs get their own directory. This enables test co-location and consistent tree structure. |
| **Test co-location** | Every doc has a paired test in `src/test/` mirror tree at the same relative path. |

One relationship breaks the strict parent chain — the **SPEC UNLOCKS FEAT** pattern (🌟).
A Specification at Layer 4 may satisfy a precondition that enables an entirely new Feature
at Layer 2. This is declared on the SPEC via `unlocks`, not on the FEAT.

## Directory Convention

The filesystem directory structure mirrors the parent-child hierarchy. Navigating the
tree with `cd` traces the traceability chain.

### Directory Naming: `Slug.class/`

Directories use **slug + class suffix** naming. No UUIDv7 on directories.
Files inside use the full `UUIDv7-Slug.class-semver.md` naming.

| Class Suffix | Layer | Example |
|:-------------|:------|:--------|
| `.strat/` | 0 | `BwrapEnhanced2026.strat/` |
| `.driv/` | 1 | `UncontainedAgentExecution.driv/` |
| `.moti/` | 1 | `NamespaceIsolatedSandbox.moti/` |
| `.feat/` | 2 | `VfsIsolation.feat/` |
| `.arch/` | 3 | `SandboxExecEngine.arch/` |
| `.spec/` | 3–4 | `ReadOnlyRoot.spec/` |

Directories are structural containers, not versioned artifacts. The `.class` suffix
self-documents the node type — `ls` instantly shows what layer each child belongs to.

### File Slug Convention

File slugs **drop the redundant class prefix** since the `.class` extension carries it:

| Old (redundant) | New (clean) |
|:----------------|:------------|
| `FeatVfsIsolation.feat-0.0.1.md` | `VfsIsolation.feat-0.0.1.md` |
| `SpecFuncReadOnlyRoot.spec-0.0.1.md` | `ReadOnlyRoot.spec-0.0.1.md` |
| `CorpStratBwrapEnhanced2026.strat-0.0.1.md` | `BwrapEnhanced2026.strat-0.0.1.md` |

### Path = Parent Chain

The filesystem path IS the traceability chain:

```
.../BwrapEnhanced2026.strat/UncontainedAgentExecution.driv/NamespaceIsolatedSandbox.moti/VfsIsolation.feat/ReadOnlyRoot.spec/
     L0 Strategy               L1 Driver                        L1 Motivator                    L2 Feature              L4 Spec
```

Every `cd ..` walks up one `parent:` pointer. Every `parent:` YAML field resolves to
the document in the directory one level up.

## Test Tree Mirroring

`src/test/{project}/` mirrors the `docs/architecture-design-features-specs/` hierarchy
exactly — same `Slug.class/` directory structure.

```
docs/.../VfsIsolation.feat/ReadOnlyRoot.spec/
    └── ReadOnlyRoot.spec-0.0.1.md              ← WHAT to test (the spec)

src/test/.../VfsIsolation.feat/ReadOnlyRoot.spec/
    └── test_readonly_root.sh                    ← HOW to test (the script)
```

**One test suite per doc.** Every SDLC document has exactly one corresponding test file.

**Test file naming:** `test_{snake_case_slug}.sh`

**Suite composition:** A parent suite runs all child suites first (depth-first),
then runs its own assertions. Results bubble up:

```bash
# Feature suite runs all child spec tests, then feature-level assertions
cd src/test/.../VfsIsolation.feat/
./test_vfs_isolation.sh
# ↑ Internally: runs ReadOnlyRoot.spec/test_readonly_root.sh,
#               runs RwEgressHome.spec/test_rw_egress_home.sh,
#               then runs feature-level assertions
```

## TDD Cycle

Each SDLC document simultaneously defines:
1. **The requirement** — what to build
2. **The test definition** — what to verify
3. **The ordering basis** — semantic content for AI dependency inference

### TDD State Machine

Every document carries a `tdd:` block in its YAML frontmatter:

```yaml
tdd:
  state: NOT_STARTED     # NOT_STARTED | RED | GREEN | REFACTOR
  test-file: null         # relative path to test script in src/test/
  last-run: null          # ISO 8601 timestamp of last test execution
  iterations: 0           # RED→GREEN cycle count
  coverage-lines: null    # source lines this node covers
```

`tdd.state` tracks the **test lifecycle** (RED/GREEN). `status` tracks the
**document lifecycle** (Draft/Active). These are independent axes:
a spec can be `Accepted` but still `RED` (approved but not yet tested).

### Assertion-Level Tracking

Test Suite tables include a `TDD` column for per-assertion status:

```markdown
| ID | Description | Method | Pass Criteria | TDD |
|----|-------------|--------|---------------|-----|
| ROR-001 | Write to /etc fails | touch /etc/test | EROFS (errno 30) | RED |
| ROR-002 | Write to /home succeeds | touch /home/u/f | exit 0 | GREEN |
```

### Depth-First Bottom-Up Workflow

The AI agent processes the hierarchy deterministically:

```python
def depth_first_tdd(node):
    for child in ai_inferred_order(node.children):  # semantic ordering
        depth_first_tdd(child)                       # leaves first

    # All children are GREEN. Process THIS node:
    doc = read(node.doc)                             # understand WHAT
    test = write(node.test_file)                     # RED — test fails
    modify(src_main_code, doc.assertions)            # minimal impl
    run(node.test_file)                              # GREEN?
    if GREEN: refactor_if_needed()
    update(doc.tdd.state = 'GREEN')
```

## Semantic Ordering

Sibling ordering is **never declared as metadata**. There are no `children-order`,
`depends-on`, or `order` fields.

### How It Works

1. **Discovery by convention:** The AI globs `*.{class}/` in the parent directory
2. **Dependency inference:** The AI reads the full content of every sibling document
   and semantically reasons about which siblings must be satisfied first
3. **Topological sort:** The AI builds a dependency DAG from inferred relationships
   and computes a topological sort
4. **Execution:** The AI processes nodes in the computed order, depth-first

### Rationale

- `depends-on` is hardcoded/static — it can drift from document content
- Document content IS the dependency declaration
- Forces documentation quality — if the AI cannot infer order, the docs are ambiguous
- Dynamic — as features evolve, inferred dependencies update automatically
- Aligns with the AI agent's core capability (semantic reasoning over technical content)

### Ties Are FATAL

If the AI cannot determine a strict ordering between two siblings, this is a
**logic FATAL** — not a tiebreak situation. The AI MUST halt and flag the ambiguity
for resolution. The fix is to improve document content until the dependency
relationship is unambiguous, NOT to add ordering metadata.
